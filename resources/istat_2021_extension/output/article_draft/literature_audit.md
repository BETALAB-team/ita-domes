# Literature revision audit

Search date: 15 September 2026. This is a targeted narrative literature check, not a systematic review or a claim of exhaustive coverage.

The revised manuscript contains 40 references (23 added to the initial 17). Five additions are from Sustainable Energy Technologies and Assessments. They are integrated into the argument alongside Energy and Buildings, Building and Environment, Applied Energy, Energy, Sustainable Cities and Society, and methodological journals. Sources were selected for an identifiable analytical role, not journal affiliation alone.

## Contribution supported by the literature

The defensible proposed contribution is the empirical assessment of whether an established Italian household representation can be retained, recalibrated or replaced when a later survey becomes available, separating changes in population composition, conditional outcomes and measurement detail before evaluating regional energy consequences. This is a repeated-cross-sectional extension of the published 2013 work, not a correction narrative or a household panel.

Existing work already establishes occupant-aware archetypes, survey-informed Italian stock models, stochastic schedules, demographic demand analysis and uncertainty-aware electrification. Accordingly, the manuscript makes no claim to be the first to introduce these components. The contribution remains conditional on completing the temporal-transfer, harmonized-decomposition, coarsening and paired-simulation experiments explicitly marked pending in the draft.

## Editorial and evidence safeguards

The 2013 published reference, empirical tables, figures and numerical results are unchanged. Methods gained citations and qualifications, not a new analysis. All 40 references are cited in the text. The original first_draft.md and first_draft.pdf are preserved.

The SETA biomass source has inconsistent units between its publisher highlights and abstract (TJ versus PJ). No numerical biomass estimate was imported. Neither retrofit savings from the Venice study nor financial results from the Irish electrification study were transferred to the Italian survey population.

The full text of some publisher papers could not be fetched directly. Their use is limited to claims supported by accessible publisher abstracts or author-institution sources. A final submission-stage review should examine full texts for methodological detail and verify the complete bibliography against the target journal's style. The existing 17 references were retained from the first-draft evidence work rather than counted as newly reviewed papers in this pass.

## Revision focus

Section 1 now distinguishes household composition, behavior, within-year schedules and between-wave change. Sections 3.2-3.9 now reference survey inference, scoring rules, drift, appliance-demand modelling, multiplicity, robust covariance and uncertainty. Sections 5.1 and 5.4-5.6 connect interpretation to aggregation, measured performance, demand timing and technology assessment. The limitations and pending-results labels remain explicit.
