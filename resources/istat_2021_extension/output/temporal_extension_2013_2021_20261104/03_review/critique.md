# Independent scientific review of the temporal extension

Reviewed 4 November 2026 before drafting the report. The supplied tables support a descriptive extension with explicit denominators and a test of how a fixed archive classifier behaves on newer observations. They do not identify time trends in Italian archetype prevalence or a change in the true number of household types. The most defensible measured changes concern equipment availability and reported hours among frequent users.

## Baseline and provenance

I checked the source PDF directly, including the plotted labels in Figure 5 on p. 7. The printed household shares are 40.1%, 26.9%, 22.9% and 10.1%. The people shares are 29.9%, 40.2%, 21.3% and 8.6%. The paragraph beside the figure states that households have equal weight. Table 3 reports demographic summaries. Table 6 reports group-specific heating hours and appliance-model electricity values. It cannot stand in for one energy value per demographic type. The analysis traces its four historical labels to the lowercase archived membership CSV. All 14 protected file hashes matched. No 2013 PCA, k-means, input mutation or simulations were run. I recomputed the means in the three archived coordinates *within already assigned labels* and replayed all 17,855 memberships with zero discrepancies. This is operational evidence for the archive proxy. The original fitted paper estimator and individual paper memberships cannot be certified from that match. [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enbuild.2025.115924)

## Independently recalculated results

I parsed the native tab-separated and caret-separated releases, verified the age and schooling codes in the native HTML classifications, and implemented a member-mask assignment from the raw fields. All 44,880 eligible IDs, weights, education indices, assignments and out-of-range flags agreed. No exact nearest-centroid ties occurred.

## Direct survey outcomes and later context

In the raw 1–5-member domains, 19,688 and 53,520 households had valid ages for every included member. This separate analysis retains young-child households. The weighted mean sizes were 2.28095 in 2013 and 2.27590 in 2021, whereas unweighted means were 2.56852 and 2.40568. The weighted share with a member under 15 fell from 22.0149% to 18.5010%, while the unweighted share rose from 16.7615% to 19.8729%. These examples show exactly why the historical unweighted comparison and survey-weighted diagnostics must remain separate.

Independent frequency and three-block reconstruction confirmed mean weighted heating hours of 7.8991 and 8.5450 among 18,332 and 49,545 eligible frequent users. Cooling was 4.8049 and 6.2625 among 2,916 and 15,654 frequent users. No included respondent reported zero total hours. All 16 archetype-conditional weighted and unweighted cells and their valid counts were also verified. These additional cells inherit proxy selection and serve as diagnostics only.

Using the broad union of cooling inventory affirmative flags in the full 1–5 domains gives 5,382/19,688 and 24,498/53,520, or 29.431% and 48.848% with the survey weights. The remaining 14,306 2013 inventory rows do not explicitly report “no” across all items. The 2013 questionnaire jumps to 2.39 after a negative response to 2.36 and explicitly prepopulates certain positive entries. The 2.36 field is absent from the local microdata header. The attached affirmative measure is checkable without asserting individual absence from blanks. In 2021 the remaining 29,022 were explicitly negative across items. Official all-household publication percentages are a stronger basis for the headline cooling time series. The 2024 official report itself names all three estimates, 29.4% in 2013, 48.8% in 2021 and 56.0% in 2024. [ISTAT 2024 report, cooling section](https://www.istat.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Report_Dotazioni-energetiche-delle-famiglie_Anno-2024.pdf)

The 2021 main/only autonomous heating share, 65.7% among households equipped with heating, is conceptually different from autonomous heating availability in 72.2% of households that year. Compare 65.8% in 2013 with 65.7% in 2021 for the main system. Compare 72.2% in 2021 with 79.0% in 2024 for availability, where systems can overlap. The 2024 release is aggregate, with no locally available household archetype labels for that wave. ## Closure, evidence and checks not performed

The initial high-priority issue was hidden differential selection. It is addressed for a **restricted-sample descriptive study** by the revised methods, quantified under-11 exclusions, explicit warning on weights, separate full-domain results and labeled review-triggered age sensitivity. It remains unresolved for any population archetype trend. The review admits the regional composition calculation, direct new survey outcomes, fixed-proxy comparison and 2021-only refit under the scopes above. The report should lead with official cooling availability and separate cross-sectional findings from conditional proxy diagnostics.

The independent scripts and their JSON evidence are stored beside this review. The frozen analysis manifest verified 40/40 artifacts, and the 14/14 protected original hashes matched. Files in the frozen analysis package are unchanged by this review. I did not recreate missing original estimators, refit 2013, reconstruct publication uncertainties, run an energy model, inspect every possible alternative age/education crosswalk or validate an unobserved respondent's type. Refit seed 42 and the full K grid were independently evaluated. The other K4 seeds and all stochastic age draws were inspected as sensitivity summaries rather than independently regenerated. The final report was not yet written at the time of scientific review.
