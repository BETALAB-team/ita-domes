# Methods and interpretation of the new extension

The immutable article benchmark is supplied in `../00_baseline/published_benchmarks.csv`. The new analysis does not estimate that benchmark again. Archive transfer takes the means of the three saved normalized coordinates conditional on the unchanged archived labels. Nearest-center replay assigns every archived record to its saved label. This is strong operational consistency, not recovery of the original fitted estimator or published per-record identity. Applying those centers to newly harmonized 2021 inputs is an **archive-calibrated assignment proxy**.

Primary 2021 inputs use midpoint ages in official age intervals (80+ bounded at 100 for this proxy), released household counts 1–5 and education codes. All actually listed members need valid ages and schooling; larger households and incomplete profiles are excluded, with record-level reasons saved. The archive education implementation contains a code-collision recoding of primary schooling to 12 years. For an archive-oriented proxy, new code 1 is mapped to that legacy value; the alternative 5-year mapping exposes its consequence. New education code 6 maps to 18 years, another approximation to an aggregated qualification category. The denominator uses a 2013-style age-class proxy to preserve the archived implementation's distinction between class codes and actual age. This proxy is not a validated education index or an exact harmonization of years studied. Age randomizations and exact-third component sensitivity do not refit 2013.

Unweighted new shares may be juxtaposed with the fixed printed 2013 percentages only with the explicit coverage warning: the published archive contains households outside the new 1–5 complete-profile domain. Regional tables are a separate new diagnostic using frozen archive labels on a restricted 2013 domain. They retain region-specific counts and denominators and do not overwrite or call themselves the article table. Geographical overlap is the intersection of represented canonical regions; demographic/education support remains different across waves and this is not a common-profile cohort. Sample composition standardization holds the 2013 archive cohort's region fractions fixed. Survey-weighted regional composition instead holds its official-weight region fractions fixed; neither constitutes a custom regional correction factor.

Weights are official final household expansion/calibration weights (2013 `coef_red`, 2021 `PESO`), as verified by the comparability workstream; no qualified repository custom factor was identified. Weighted 2021 shares are shown separately from the paper's unweighted estimand. Effective sample size is `(sum weight)^2 / sum(weight^2)`, not an expansion count treated as independent observations. No design-based confidence intervals are claimed because compatible strata/cluster design and harmonization uncertainty have not been recovered.

Exploratory 2021 clustering fits only new observations. A bounded NumPy Lloyd implementation uses 10 starts, at most 150 iterations per start, tolerance 1e-8, and seeds 0, 42 and 2021 for K4. It operates on the frozen archived coordinate scale. Matching enumerates all 24 one-to-one assignments and minimizes summed squared distances between new and frozen centroids: the same optimum as a Hungarian assignment for K4. ARI/NMI compare the resulting partition to the fixed proxy; they are partition agreement, not validation. Candidate K=2–8 has a deterministic silhouette evaluation sample of 1500; this was narrowed from the prespecified ceiling of 5000 for bounded memory. This exploration cannot change the four immutable published types.

Only actual data underpin the figures. SVG and PNG bars use identical numerical values and geometry, and image dimensions are checked when written. The bundled runtime lacks compatible scipy/sklearn/matplotlib; direct NumPy calculations and Pillow rendering avoid environment changes. Source hashes are checked before and after execution. Both PNG plots were visually checked for readable titles, labels, geometry and data labels; the footer encoding was corrected. Independent review also verifies numeric tables.

The primary complete-profile cohort is materially selective: 2,798 of 10,990 households reporting an under-15 member are eligible, compared with 42,082 of 43,079 without an under-15 member. These are raw-release coverage diagnostics, including the household-size exclusion. Consequently the new shares cannot support a national 2013–2021 temporal prevalence conclusion; they describe a selected, harmonized cohort under an imperfect archived education mapping. Regional standardization adjusts geography, not this selection mechanism. The missingness audit is in `coverage_under15_2021.csv` and cohort size/macroregion tables.


Results: the selected 2021 profile cohort has 44,880 records, from 54,069 released households (549 size6+ and 8,640 incomplete/unsupported profiles). Under-11 schooling is structurally unasked, not ordinary item nonresponse: all 7,803 size1–5 households with an under-11 child are necessarily omitted, representing 16.02% of that cohort under final survey weights. The remaining omitted profiles add further selection. The profile estimand is **complete-profile size1–5 households without children under11**, not the full national household universe.

Under the primary midpoint proxy, A1–4 shares are 27.61/33.92/27.42/11.05%, versus the unchanged printed 40.1/26.9/22.9/10.1%. This juxtaposition mixes coverage and cannot establish a temporal cluster-prevalence shift. Separate 2021 survey-weighted proxy shares are 30.47/29.15/31.88/8.50%. Geography-standardized diagnostic changes are -12.79/+7.42/+4.44/+0.93 percentage points against the new size-restricted archived-label diagnostic, but geographic standardization cannot repair missing household types or education harmonization.

The aligned exploratory 2021 K4 refit (seed42) yields 31.64/25.19/26.68/16.50%, with ARI0.6694/NMI0.6609 against the fixed transfer proxy. Seed0/2021 variation is modest. However, the prespecified sampled-silhouette exploration prefers K2 (0.408) to K4 (0.345). The refit is exploratory structure in the selected new cohort, not a newly validated national taxonomy.

Age draws change approximately 7.9–8.1% of proxy assignments; the primary-school-years alternative changes 2.41%, and the new-coordinate SD distance metric changes 2.81%. There are 2,985 observations outside at least one archived coordinate range (6.65%); retaining only in-range observations changes shares to 22.81/36.32/29.37/11.50%. These sensitivities substantially limit interpretations of raw prevalence differences. The review-triggered age alignment sensitivity, added after independent review rather than represented as preregistered, replaces only representative ages with the expected archived age-imputation value within each new released age interval and holds education fixed. Its separate table preserves the primary result.

For a broader source-grounded extension, direct survey diagnostics use all size1–5 households (19,688 in 2013 and 53,520 in 2021), independent of cluster assignment. Official weighting changes the substantive interpretation: mean size is almost stable at 2.28095 versus 2.27590, while the unweighted mean changes 2.56852 to 2.40568. Weighted households with an under-15 member change from 22.01% to 18.50%; the unweighted figures instead rise 16.76% to 19.87%. These are newly computed descriptive diagnostics, never replacements for the article's archived cluster table. Regional rows retain raw region-code systems and an explicit canonical column; 2021 provinces41/42 must be pooled by denominator, not by averaging percentages, before comparing canonical region4.

Conditional heating/cooling means are new descriptive diagnostics using frequency1/2 and all three valid0–8 hour blocks, with separate valid counts and cohort denominators. 2013 refers to a typical winter/summer day and 2021 to cold/hot months of2020. These are not a weather-normalized trend, metered consumption, appliance kWh or immutable published H/E group means. The AppendixA projection was omitted because apartment-size versus floor distinctions prevent a defensible full mapping; no new forced H4/E5 groups were fitted.

The post-review age alignment gives A1–4 shares32.50/30.03/23.61/13.87%, changing10.18% of labels. In particular A3 is only0.71 percentage points above the immutable printed22.9% under this plausible alternative. This reinforces that apparent cluster-share shifts cannot be separated from crosswave age/education harmonization by the present data alone.

Broad cooling affirmative inventories are source-comparable after collapsing the 2013 central/autonomous pair into the corresponding 2021 whole-home category. On the full size1–5 cohort, affirmative fractions are27.34%→45.77% unweighted, and29.43%→48.85% survey weighted. The literal all-household denominator is retained. The2013 nonaffirmative inventory is routed blank, so it is labeled unknown/no affirmative, never automatically asserted to be observed absence;2021 has explicit no responses. This evidence supports an increase in reported cooling equipment availability, with questionnaire/routing qualifications. It does not measure cooling energy or efficiency.
