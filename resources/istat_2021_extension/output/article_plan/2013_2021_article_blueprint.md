# Do household energy archetypes travel through time?

## A full-length article blueprint

**Recommended title:** *Temporal transferability of household energy archetypes: evidence from Italy's 2013 and 2021 surveys and implications for regional building-stock modelling*

**Alternative, if the retrofit experiments are completed:** *Updating household energy archetypes for regional retrofit assessment: a repeated cross-sectional study of Italy, 2013–2021*

Prepared 14 September 2026. This is a structure and research design for an approximately 11,000-word article, not a completed manuscript or a report of new simulation results. Proposed findings below are questions to test, not assumed outcomes.

## 1. The paper we should write

The central question is not simply how Italian households differed between the two surveys. It is **when an established representation of households remains useful for building-energy modelling, when it needs updating, and whether that update changes an energy-planning decision**.

The two existing papers provide complementary foundations. The 2013-survey article establishes household archetypes and their connections with dwelling characteristics and energy-related use. The CLIMA chapter supplies the regional, physics-based modelling framework. The new article connects these foundations through an explicit temporal transfer experiment, then follows the consequences from household distributions to regional energy estimates and retrofit assessment.

Suggested contribution statement:

> Building on survey-derived Italian household archetypes and the ita-domes residential stock model, this study evaluates the temporal transferability of household representations between the 2013 and 2021 ISTAT surveys. It distinguishes changing population composition from changes in conditional operating patterns, quantifies the consequences of differing survey detail, and tests whether refreshing household inputs changes regional energy estimates and the relative performance of retrofit options.

This is a proposed, defensible positioning based on a targeted literature review. It is not a substantiated claim to be the first study worldwide to combine these elements.

### What the CLIMA chapter actually establishes

The supplied PDF contains the full target chapter on printed pages 1302–1311. It describes EUReCA-based lumped-capacitance calculations, survey-informed operation, appliance calculations using external performance data, and geographically differentiated weather. Its results comprise national energy-balance comparisons for 2013/2014 and regional maps by end use and carrier. Historical weather replaces typical-year inputs for 30 provinces representing approximately 27% of households. Although retrofit assessment motivates the framework, the results section does not present a retrofit scenario comparison. Thus, the new work can add a controlled temporal-input experiment and a demonstrated decision application. Cite this as a **2026 publication in the CLIMA 2025 proceedings**. [Vivian et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-10546-2_123)

### Relationship to the published 2013 article

Treat the published study as the empirical foundation, not as an object of criticism. Its four-archetype representation gives the new article a substantive reference system rather than an arbitrary starting point. [Khajedehi et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enbuild.2025.115924)

Keep two clearly labelled layers:

1. **Published 2013 benchmarks:** reproduce cited values exactly where needed, with original definitions and denominators. Do not replace these with later repository outputs.
2. **New harmonized cross-wave analysis:** apply a common measurement and estimation specification to both survey waves. This answers a new temporal question; it must not silently mix published estimates with differently constructed 2021 estimates.

Suggested bridge paragraph:

> The earlier study established an empirical representation of Italian household diversity for building-energy applications. The availability of a subsequent survey makes it possible to examine the persistence and changing prevalence of these profiles. Here, the analysis moves from identifying household archetypes at one observation point to assessing their transferability across survey waves and their implications for regional energy modelling.

Use “extends,” “updates,” “evaluates transferability,” and “common cross-wave specification.” There is no need for a correction narrative or a correction register in this article.

## 2. Reference check and novelty boundary

### Search scope and strength of evidence

The targeted check covered Italian stock models and their predecessors, national-survey-derived archetypes, occupant-aware stock simulation, temporal household composition, and decomposition methods. It followed the two anchor papers' references and checked relevant work through September 2026 using publisher, conference, official-statistics and institutional sources. Both anchor papers were available in full; several related papers were assessed through abstracts, institutional summaries or indexed passages rather than a complete full-text review. This is not a systematic review and does not warrant an exhaustive absence claim.

**Recommended novelty hierarchy:** temporal predictive transfer is the primary methodological contribution; measurement-detail sensitivity makes that comparison credible; regional simulation and retrofit-ranking stability demonstrate its practical value. The demographic decomposition explains the results rather than carrying the entire novelty claim.

Before submission, obtain and read complete texts of the closest comparator papers, especially the MOIRAE socio-demographic extension and the recent Spanish studies. Replace any broad gap statement that does not survive that check. Retain a source-by-claim literature ledger, not just a long bibliography.

## 3. Research questions and tests

| Question | Main estimand or comparison | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| RQ1. How transferable is the household representation? | Changes in weighted archetype shares, within-archetype profiles, and out-of-reference support. | A frozen common demographic transformation and assignment rule, uncertainty estimates and support diagnostics. |
| RQ2. What contributes to cross-wave differences? | Composition versus conditional-outcome components for heating operation, cooling and standardized appliance electricity. | Common definitions, survey weights, comparable denominators and a decomposition that does not label residual change as causal behavior. |
| RQ3. What is the value of updating? | Untouched-2021 predictive performance of frozen 2013, recalibrated, and newly fitted models. | Common test households, information sets, outcomes and predeclared metrics. |
| RQ4. Does updating matter for energy-planning conclusions? | Paired changes in regional demand and retrofit savings/rankings under alternative household representations. | The same physical stock, weather, intervention definitions and model settings across comparisons. |

These tests must permit a finding of stability. An update that barely changes regional totals but alters subgroup distributions is meaningful; so is evidence that an established representation remains adequate within specified uncertainty.

## 4. Proposed article structure: 11,000 words

The budget below includes the abstract and main narrative, but excludes references, table contents, captions and supplementary material. Adjust to the eventual journal's counting rules. It is a drafting budget, not a reason to pad sections.

| Section | Words | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Abstract | 250 | State the temporal question, independent survey waves, transfer experiment, principal quantified findings when available, and practical implication. |
| 1. Introduction | 1,150 | Motivate representation ageing, establish the two foundations, narrow the literature gap, state four questions and contributions. |
| 2. Data and cross-wave comparability | 1,400 | Define populations, reference periods, weights, harmonization, observed versus inferred quantities and analysis-specific samples. |
| 3. Analytical and simulation framework | 2,450 | Explain archetype transfer, decomposition, electricity reconstruction, insulation/cooling analysis, physical experiments and uncertainty. |
| 4. Results | 3,150 | Answer the four questions in order; conclude with robustness and decision relevance. |
| 5. Discussion | 1,350 | Interpret the value of updating, compare literature, explain policy relevance and limitations. |
| 6. Conclusions | 450 | Give a direct answer to each question and conditions for reusing or updating the archetypes. |
| Reproducibility and data availability | 150 | Describe versioned code, parameter provenance, seeds, derived outputs and microdata access restrictions. |
| **Total** | **11,000** | |

### 1. Introduction — 1,150 words

**1.1 Household representation as a time-sensitive modelling input — 300.** Explain why household size, age composition, equipment ownership and operation matter independently of the physical envelope. A household representation fitted once may remain interpretable while losing predictive calibration.

**1.2 Two complementary foundations — 250.** Position the published archetype paper and ita-domes as linked contributions. Avoid repeating their entire methods. Distinguish household archetypes from physical building archetypes: the two classifications serve different purposes and must not be conflated.

**1.3 What remains to be tested — 400.** Organize the literature around empirical transfer, changing population composition, and downstream decision sensitivity. Explicitly acknowledge existing demographic stock scenarios and survey-informed simulations. Explain why harmonization is integral to a valid temporal test.

**1.4 Aim and contribution — 200.** Present RQ1–RQ4 and a short roadmap. Do not announce an expected direction of change as a finding.

### 2. Data and cross-wave comparability — 1,400 words

**2.1 Survey populations, collection and reference periods — 350.** Describe each survey separately before defining the common domain. Record collection year and reference period for every operational/expenditure outcome. Some 2021-wave questions refer to 2020, as the official release makes clear. The pandemic is therefore an interpretation issue, not a footnote. [ISTAT, 2020–2021 release](https://www.istat.it/comunicato-stampa/consumi-energetici-famiglie-anni-2020-2021/)

**2.2 Harmonization of household, dwelling and equipment information — 450.** Build a semantic crosswalk from both tracciati and questionnaires, checked against the 2021 report workbooks. Classify variables as equivalent, comparable after aggregation, more detailed, new, or unavailable for a particular construct. A renamed code is not sufficient evidence of equivalence. Preserve response universes, units, time windows and skip rules.

**2.3 Outcomes and energy boundaries — 350.** Define observed heating hours and cooling indicators separately from reconstructed kWh. Separate appliance electricity from cooling, heating, cooking and DHW. If expenditure-derived quantities are retained, document tariffs, fixed charges and reference periods; do not present them as metered consumption.

**2.4 Samples, weights and missing information — 250.** Show raw, common-domain and outcome-specific sample counts and weighted coverage. Existing analyses restrict the demographic domain to households of one to five people with required information. National all-household claims require an explicit treatment of excluded households and calibration to appropriate population totals.

### 3. Analytical and simulation framework — 2,450 words

**3.1 Frozen-reference archetypes and temporal support — 400.** Fit transformations and reference clusters on 2013 training data, freeze them, and assign both waves within the same space. Compare weighted shares, profile distributions and distances from the reference support. Use a newly fitted 2021 clustering only as a sensitivity analysis, with explicit label alignment. Cluster labels from independent fits are not automatically comparable.

**3.2 Cross-wave transfer and recalibration — 350.** Compare: T0, frozen 2013 conditional models; T1, the same archetype structure with conditional models recalibrated on 2021 training data; T2, a 2021-specific representation and conditional models. Reserve the same 2021 test subset for all three and finish tuning before inspecting it. Evaluate categorical dwelling predictions with weighted log loss, Brier scores and calibration; continuous operation with MAE and distribution/quantile diagnostics. Report regional and archetype-specific performance where supported.

**3.3 Decomposition of population-level differences — 300.** For a mean outcome, let s(g,t) be the weighted share of common archetype g and m(g,t) its conditional mean. Use the symmetric identity:

    change = Σg [s(g,21) − s(g,13)] × [m(g,21) + m(g,13)] / 2
           + Σg [m(g,21) − m(g,13)] × [s(g,21) + s(g,13)] / 2

The first term is composition; the second is conditional-outcome change. This is descriptive, not causal. Changes in technology, unobserved circumstances, weather and reporting can enter the second term. For national energy totals, additionally distinguish household-number changes. The classical decomposition provides a transparent starting point; distributional counterfactual methods are optional if tails become central. [Kitagawa, 1955](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1955.10501299); [Chernozhukov et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA10582)

**3.4 Appliance reconstruction and the survey-detail experiment — 350.** Make the main cross-wave indicator a standardized electricity requirement under common reference efficiencies and the same accessible inputs. Coarsen 2013 appliance information to the 2021 information set, then rerun the calculation. Comparing rich-input and coarsened-input 2013 estimates quantifies the sensitivity to information loss while holding households constant. This is distinct from real temporal change. A second, historically parameterized track is possible only with verified period-specific efficiencies; uncertainty in those parameters must be propagated. Model predictions of modeled kWh are not validation against observed electricity consumption.

**3.5 Cooling and insulation — 300.** Separate AC ownership, use frequency and conditional duration. Respect questionnaire routing and distinguish non-use from nonresponse. For insulation, the 2013 subjective quality rating and a recent insulation/window intervention are different constructs. The 2021 combined improvement field must not be relabelled as a measured present-day insulation state. Use a harmonized intervention variable only if the questionnaire time window and included measures match after aggregation. Exclude unknown responses from this particular comparison, retain eligible records for unrelated analyses, and report exclusions. Heating-group boxplots should use two colors for known yes/no categories, annotated with effect estimates, confidence intervals and multiplicity-adjusted p-values. Where the construct is improvement, label it “Insulation/window improvement: yes/no.”

**3.6 Coupling and controlled simulation experiments — 450.** Retain the ita-domes physical approach and make the household-input interface explicit: probabilities, heating/cooling operation, appliance consumption and internal gains. Preserve dependencies between households, buildings and equipment through conditional assignment, rather than independently shuffling incompatible attributes. The minimum experiment holds a 2021 physical stock and common weather fixed and contrasts standard inputs, 2013 household inputs and 2021 household inputs. Add a 2×2 household-vintage/stock-vintage experiment only when both physical stock representations are sufficiently supported. Do not infer U-values directly from a recent-improvement flag. Use the same intervention eligibility and engineering assumptions for envelope improvement, heating-system replacement and their combination.

**3.7 Validation and uncertainty — 300.** Separate historical validation using appropriately dated weather and energy balances from weather-controlled counterfactual comparisons. Match sectors, carriers, end uses and population coverage. National aggregate agreement does not independently validate household behavior; total residential electricity cannot directly validate an appliances-only estimate. Propagate sampling, missing-detail and parameter uncertainty separately. Use survey-design information when available; otherwise disclose that robust/model-based intervals do not reproduce the full complex-sample design. Predefine comparisons and adjustment families.

### 4. Results — 3,150 words

**4.1 What can be compared, and who is represented? — 350.** Open with the common information domain, exclusions and weighted coverage. Keep code-level detail in the supplement.

**4.2 Persistence and changing prevalence of archetypes — 500.** Present profiles and shares together. Ask whether changes occur mainly in prevalence or within-profile characteristics. Show unsupported regions of feature space rather than forcing all assignments to appear equally representative.

**4.3 Heating, cooling and appliance-related differences — 650.** Combine comparable weighted distributions with the two-color insulation/improvement panels. Present AC ownership separately from conditional operation. Show standardized appliance components and the coarsening sensitivity. Avoid turning a long inventory of pairwise p-values into the narrative.

**4.4 Composition versus conditional-outcome change — 400.** Present the decomposition nationally and in supported regional groupings. Explain any cancellation between components; an unchanged national mean can conceal substantial opposing shifts.

**4.5 How much is gained by updating? — 400.** Compare T0–T2 on untouched test data with uncertainty. Identify whether recalibrating the existing structure captures most of the gain or whether a different representation is warranted.

**4.6 Regional energy and retrofit consequences — 550.** Report paired model differences under common stock/weather first, then intervention savings and rankings. Distinguish total regional savings from per-dwelling savings. Frame rank changes as sensitivity to representation, not proof of which policy is causally optimal.

**4.7 Robustness — 300.** Summarize whether the headline conclusion survives information-detail assumptions, weights, common-domain restrictions, missingness choices, stochastic assignment and weather coverage. Put exhaustive diagnostics in the supplement.

### 5. Discussion — 1,350 words

**5.1 What temporal transferability means in practice — 350.** Distinguish stable interpretation, predictive calibration and stable simulation decisions. An archetype can satisfy one but not the others.

**5.2 Contribution relative to existing literature — 350.** Return to the closest comparators, not generic clustering literature. Explain the empirical update test and how its effects propagate into physical simulations. Do not suggest that demographic effects, occupant-aware models or electrification scenarios were previously absent.

**5.3 Implications for regional planning and model maintenance — 300.** Discuss which inputs require refreshing, where common profiles remain useful, and where data collection has the highest practical value. Present conditional recommendations supported by observed performance, not an invented universal refresh interval.

**5.4 Limits and next observations — 350.** State the repeated cross-sectional design, pandemic/reference-period issue, changing instruments, modeled electricity, uncertain envelope properties, geographically incomplete inputs and sample-domain limits. Two waves cannot identify a secular trend independently of a period shock. A later wave can test persistence; do not expand this article to 2024 microdata without checking access and comparability.

### 6. Conclusions — 450 words

Answer the four research questions in the same order. Give quantified results only after analysis. End with a practical statement about the circumstances under which inherited household representations can be reused, recalibrated or replaced. If regional rankings remain stable, report that finding directly rather than forcing a claim that updating always changes decisions.

## 9. Final framing safeguards

- “2013–2021” identifies the survey-wave comparison. State the actual reference period alongside each outcome.
- Call this a repeated cross-sectional temporal comparison, or population-level longitudinal perspective, never a household panel.
- Unknown insulation/improvement responses are excluded only from that analysis; unknown never means no.
- A reported improvement is not proof of current envelope performance, and its association with heating use is not a causal renovation effect.
- Common-efficiency modeled electricity is a standardized indicator, not a measured historical electricity trend.
- Published 2013 values remain intact; new harmonized values are explicitly new estimates.
- The main scientific result must be a test of transfer and consequences, not a predetermined claim of deterioration or improvement.
- All new work stays off main. This blueprint creates no commits and changes no model code.
