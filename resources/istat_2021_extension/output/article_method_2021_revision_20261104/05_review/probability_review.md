# Independent probability-fit review

I verified the underlying historical evidence against the original graphic, saved plotting script and input CSV. The recovered Git source also specifies the 5-year × 24-m² centre increments and inclusive ±15-year and ±40-m² counting windows. The new comparisons recover this geometry. They do not claim a literal rerun of the historical notebook state. See the accompanying paper method check for the caption and source discrepancy.

Starting with the unchanged 46,712 drawing-and-label records, I rebuilt counts with a three-dimensional cumulative histogram and rectangular difference counts. All 125 primary window counts and empirical four-way proportions matched. The smallest cell has 720 observations, the largest 17,737. Their counts sum to 691,988 because a household can contribute to multiple overlapping windows. Neither this total nor 125 window observations is an independent household sample.

I fitted the six-term quadratic independently with four statsmodels regressions on exactly the same design and independently fitted the row-specific backward specifications. All 24 full-precision primary coefficients match the recommended matrix. The constant coefficients sum to 1; all five nonconstant columns sum to zero to machine precision. With a common full-rank design and intercept, ordinary least squares necessarily predicts sums of 1 when its four training responses do. Positivity is a separate property. I analytically checked stationary points, edge extrema and the four corners of the continuous age 15–135 years × area 40–136 m² application rectangle. The resulting ranges are A1 0.138–0.363, A2 0.221–0.447, A3 0.135–0.334, A4 0.148–0.283. Beyond that domain 73 of the 225 evaluated wider-area nodes have at least one invalid probability. The original coefficient matrix is unchanged.

I independently regenerated each 70/30 split, rebuilding both training and held-out windows before fitting or evaluation. On the *same 125-window held-out target*, the coarse 21-window backward model scored a mean RMSE of 0.040465, the dense-window backward model scored 0.022560, and the recommended dense full quadratic scored 0.022556. The dense full fit improved over the coarse fit in every split, giving a 44.26% aggregate reduction in this held-out **window-proportion** RMSE. This is not individual-level classification or panel validation. The coarse and dense backward vs. full comparisons show that almost all of the measured improvement comes with the recovered grid and broader training windows. The full basis adds internally coherent probability sums. Its tiny error advantage over dense backward selection does not by itself establish statistical superiority. With strong overlap and model selection on the same repeated splits, confidence intervals treating these windows or splits as independent population replicates would be inappropriate.

All 20 observed and fitted vintage-by-area summary nodes agree when both are equally averaged over the exact same age-centre nodes. The vintage labels describe **centres**, since ±15-year windows span boundaries. The retained 2021 model is national, pooled over dwelling type, macroregion and other characteristics. It captures area dependence by age through age×area and nonlinear terms. Avoid describing it as a regional- or dwelling-type-specific fit. The changed source vintage cut at 1950 respects the released pre-1950 category. Additional context can be introduced only as a separately estimated model.

Independent checks and numerical evidence are in check_probability.py and probability_check.json. The frozen 2013 benchmark and the preceding 2021 results were read-only.
