# Diagnosis, validation and recommendation

The revision should use the full six-term quadratic probability matrix fitted on the recovered historical 125-node geometry, within age15–135 years and area40–136 m2. It restores the original study's actual empirical-regression context and retains a compact matrix. More elaborate curves are not justified by the heldout comparisons.

The main error was upstream of polynomial fitting. The prior run used seven age centers and three primary area centers with narrow6year/8m2half-windows, giving21regression rows. The saved original2013input has25age centers and five area centers, giving125rows after the original filter; the historical generator uses broad overlapping15year/40m2half-windows. The paper's actualFigure6 plots dwellingarea horizontally with vintage curves, although its caption describes a contradictory orientation. We inspected the actual graphic and recovered the source generator from read-only Git history. No2013regression or cluster table was refitted as a replacement.

The fair comparison holds the validation target fixed. For each of five70/30household splits, fit the old21window backward model on training households and evaluate it on the same125node heldout proportions used for the new125model. MeanRMSE declines from.040465 to.022556 (44.26%); count-weightedRMSE declines from.036304 to.020770. Every test-window proportion is built from heldout households only. Demographic labels remain fixed from the previous fit, so this assesses the conditional probability stage rather than external end-to-end prediction. The overlapping windows reuse households and the five splits reuse the release; no independent-window significance test or confidence interval is claimed.

With the correct125geometry, backward elimination and fullquadratic have essentially identical heldoutRMSE (.022560 and.022556). Thus it would be misleading to attribute improvement chiefly to removal of p-value selection. Correcting the window geometry is decisive. Keeping the common fullbasis is preferable because it preserves the sum-to-one identity without materially worsening performance. The original05_03restricted basis hasRMSE.022893; the printed paper's group-specific sparse masks haveRMSE.022822. The joint multinomial quadratic hasRMSE.023724 in this primarydomain and adds a logit transformation without improving accuracy. All-area fits are assessed separately and should not silently replace the original restricted-domain estimand.

The recommended all-data matrix has125fit nodes. ApparentA1–A4RMSE is.01847/.02022/.01009/.01014 and adjustedR² is.90105/.85526/.94616/.91308. These are fits to overlapping smoothed proportions, not evidence of household classification accuracy or building-energy accuracy. Dense evaluation at every integer age15–135 and area40–136 yields no individual probability outside0–1 and sum errors only at floating-point precision. Wider-domain predictions are supplied with explicit extrapolation and range flags; do not extrapolate the matrix as if it were a validated categorical distribution.

Original-style vintage profiles use construction-era labels of the AGE-WINDOW CENTERS, not strict membership-era prevalence. In each vintage, empirical and fitted points average exactly the same age-center nodes with identical equal weights at each of40/64/88/112/136m2. Continuous lines use those same fixed nodes. The age windows themselves span30years and can cross era boundaries. This preserves matched truth and prediction context and avoids arbitrary representative ages.
