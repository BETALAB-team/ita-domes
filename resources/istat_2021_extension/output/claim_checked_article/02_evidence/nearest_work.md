# Closest work and defensible extension boundary

Owner: literature worker. Evidence accessed 20 October 2026. This is a targeted narrative comparison, not a systematic review. Scientific summaries and exact locators are in `sources.json`; citation metadata in `bibliography.bib`. Source identifiers are stable.

The selected CLIMA chapter remains the foundation. Its published national and regional results must be described through the conference owner's complete chapter audit. The 2025 demographic-archetype article is a distinct related contribution, rather than a replacement foundation. Its user identification, dwelling association and survey-derived patterns cannot be claimed again as a new result.

Frozen primary question (orchestrator decision): **How sensitive are illustrative EUReCA heating outputs to seeded contiguous, earliest and latest within-block placement of heating availability, preserving the actual reported Veneto2013 morning/afternoon/night hours, first under Padova weather and then under2–4contrasting weather files?** The baseline is the inspected current GRINS construction; it must not be called the reproduced conference schedule without provenance.

This is an experimental question, not an established literature gap. Its contribution is specifically the effect of unobserved within-block placement conditional on fixed reported duration, fixed illustrative physical case and explicit reconstruction rules. Earliest/latest are design alternatives within the tested construction, not proven extrema over all feasible schedules or probabilistic uncertainty bounds. A null difference remains useful if it bounds sensitivity for the tested constructions. Padova weather alone establishes no city-representative population. Without independent measurements, the answer concerns simulated sensitivity and robustness, never demonstrated predictive improvement.

Secondary questions can separate annual totals from load timing and ask whether variation from household mapping is distinguishable from envelope/HVAC assumptions. An hourly schedule built from survey hours requires an explicit construction and uncertainty range; it is not observed hourly behaviour. Keep energy demand, delivered energy and appliance electricity boundaries separate.

The especially close precedent is SRC-UA2022 Section3.1.1 p6: it uses the same three ISTATtime bands, creates a random profile for each respondent, averages these into a reference schedule, then samples total operating hours and shifts start times within its broader uncertainty analysis. Therefore even random reconstruction from these bands is already published. The selected extension isolates one previously bundled modelling choice; it is not a new survey schedule generation method. SRC-KOENE2023 separately demonstrates existing RC-model work on temporal thermostat dispersion and district heating peaks. SRC-HUEBNER2013 motivates timing heterogeneity from observed English temperature data, without validating an Italian reconstruction.

The 2013–2021 direction should be optional until the harmonization audit. The official Istat MFR page lists both waves, but publication availability does not establish comparable variables or support individual transitions. Repeated surveys do not track households; wave, technology, weather and questionnaire changes cannot be attributed exclusively to behaviour. Do not merge this second direction into the pilot merely to enlarge the paper.

Permitted novelty wording before results: **We assess the consequences of specified household-input choices in a documented extension of the CLIMA model, using matched simulations and controlled weather contrasts.** After results, replace “assess” with the actual bounded finding and state the cases, sites and endpoints. Avoid “first,” “fills the gap,” “more accurate,” “validated nationally,” and “national transferability” unless separate evidence warrants each statement.
