## Findings

### R1-01 — Critical: timestep mode switching violates the network state balance

**Claims:** MET-003, MET-005, RES-004, INT-001; dependent gate statements in RQ-002/MET-006, and quantitative temperature interpretation in LIM-005.

**Exact evidence:** `C:/Works/EUReCA/EUReCA/eureca_building/thermal_zone.py`, `solve_timestep`, lines 1609–1648 and state updates around 1777–1789; v1 `adapter_snapshot.py` loop calling `z.solve_timestep(h, weather, flag=z.flag, model='1C')`. Reviewer diagnostic paths are listed above.

When the initial mode is off and heating becomes necessary, lines 1610–1613 recursively call `solve_timestep`. The inner call commits `Ta0` and `Tm0`. Control then returns to the outer call, whose separate `if self.flag == 'heating on'` block executes again from that already updated state. This advances the thermal response twice within one nominal hour. In the opposite heating-to-off transition, the outer call receives returned air temperature/load but retains incompatible outer-frame surface/mass variables, which are then used in its final state update. Both paths can produce states inconsistent with one timestep of the declared discrete equations.

The independently evaluated balances use the **original beginning-of-hour state**, actual gains and conductances, actual infiltration and output temperatures/load, with the same 3600-second step. The zero-ventilation and zero-cooling conditions of this pilot remove ambiguous additional terms. At a 1e-6 W diagnostic tolerance:

| Case | Hours with a failing node balance | Largest absolute node residual |
|---|---:|---:|
| Seeded | 898 | 9261.722 W |
| Early | 846 | 9647.828 W |
| Late | 766 | 9381.844 W |

For example, seeded hour 14 changes off to heating, returns 356.420 W and 19°C, but leaves a mass-node residual of 2242.561 W relative to the beginning-of-hour mass temperature. Seeded hour 13 changes heating to off and leaves air/surface residuals of −316.051/+836.058 W. These are not floating-point tolerance effects. Exact repeated results and identical warm-up outcomes reproduce the defect.

**Consequence:** CSV arithmetic remains true as a record of what this executable returned, but the claimed annual/peak sensitivity cannot be interpreted as a verified hourly thermal response. This issue is especially material because the experiment changes switching times, exactly where the defect operates. A direct call to `sensible_balance_1C` passing its own equations does not test this dispatcher. Do not inherit the existing pilot gate into further results using the same solver path. No assertion is made here about the magnitude or sign of corrected results.

**Checkable resolution:** Quarantine affected numerical claims now. A simulation owner should implement a local, documented transition fix or alternate adapter under the artifact root, preserving external checkouts and archived runs. Every candidate mode must use the same beginning-of-step state, and only the accepted solution may commit state once. Add meaningful off→heating and heating→off transition checks, plus per-step air/surface/mass residual checks over the annual cases. Declare tolerances from numerical scale/precision, retain full maximum residuals, and show invariance to the prior mode where the physical state/inputs are identical. Rerun the three contrasts, exact repeat and warm-up test, update run hashes and claims, and have the correction independently reviewed. If a fix cannot be completed, retain explicit pending simulation results rather than claim a completed sensitivity finding.

### R1-02 — Major: the preserved binary durations are high-setpoint hours, not enforced heating-operation hours

**Claims:** GAP-001, RQ-001, MET-001, MET-002, RES-003, RES-004, INT-001.

**Exact evidence:** Official questionnaire pp41–42 asks operating/on-hours. `EndUse.py` lines 599–608 maps the selected binary profile to 22/19°C. `thermal_zone.py` lines 1474–1478 enables heating regardless of that binary profile; the adapter passes a temperature schedule, not equipment availability. Saved `seeded_hourly.csv` has positive heating at 19°C in 801 hours, contributing 1220.322315 kWh; early and late have 601/750 such hours. See `reviewer_01/independent_checks.json`. These diagnostic quantities describe the defective archived execution and must not be promoted as corrected findings.

The draft usefully discloses the assumed 22/19°C values, so the mapping is not hidden. However, repeated descriptions of “on/off sequence”, “on-hours” and “duration-preserving” do not explicitly identify what the solver actually preserves. In the executable, the four/four/one hours are periods at the higher thermostat setting. Heating remains available during the other hours at the setback temperature. This distinction is central to the estimand, not merely a label: a strict availability experiment would be a different experiment.

**Consequence:** A reader could infer that equal reported operation durations constrain actual modeled heating-operation duration. They do not. The pilot tests timing of a two-level thermostat construction conditional on an additional survey-to-setpoint assumption.

**Checkable resolution:** State explicitly in the research question, method and result wording that the alternatives preserve the binary input/high-setpoint duration, while allowing setback heating outside those periods; neither actual heating-on duration nor energy delivery duration is preserved. Keep the original survey wording when describing the survey, then distinguish the model transformation. A schematic should label 22°C/19°C setpoints rather than equipment on/off. Relabeling and an explicit limitation are sufficient for the present narrow study; a genuine on/off experiment would require a separately frozen design and new runs.

### R1-03 — Minor: measurement evidence class assigned to absence of measurements

**Claims:** DAT-004; machine-readable claim ledgers.

**Exact evidence:** DAT-004 has `Evidence class: observed measurement`, while its exact claim and provenance concern an unsuccessful local inventory for a compatible meter series. `04_analysis/audit_inputs.py` collects GIS property keys, not independently documented heating measurements.

**Consequence:** Automated evidence summaries can wrongly count the study as containing observed-measurement evidence, despite the scientifically correct limitation text.

**Checkable resolution:** Change this evidence class to `derived estimate` (with an inventory/audit qualifier), or introduce a documented audit class consistently in the readable and machine schemas. Maintain the limited-directory scope and avoid asserting that measurements do not exist elsewhere.
