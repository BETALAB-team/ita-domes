of the Italian Residential Building Stock

Abstract. In this paper, we present a bottom-up model of the Italian residential 
building stock called ita-domes. The model uses data from an ISTA T survey on 
the energy consumption of families to account for the behavioral and technolog-
ical diversity in energy use within households. The model accounts for different 
climates within each region. V alidation against National Energy Balances of 2013 
and 2014 showed alignment for natural gas, electricity and biomass consump-
tion but discrepancies for LPG, gasoline and district heating. The paper shows 
that the model can provide estimates on the effect of energy efﬁciency measures 
with regional resolution, thus being a useful tool to inform policy makers. The 
model is released as an open-source Github repository. Future work will update 
this analysis using surveys conducted by ISTA T in 2021 and 2024. 
Keywords: building stock · national model · policy support 
1 Introduction 
In recent years, there has been growing attention towards the decarbonization of the 
building sector, driven by the need to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and align with 
global climate goals. EU policies such as the European Green Deal [ 1], the Energy 
Performance of Buildings Directive [2], and the Fit for 55 packages [3] have established 
ambitious targets to improve building energy efﬁciency and promote renewable energy 
integration. Building simulations emerged as a powerful tool to estimate the effect of 
energy efﬁciency at a large scale, providing valuable insights to achieve the European 
target goals. The reliability of such simulations does not only depend on the accuracy of 
the underlying models, but also on the quality of input data including weather conditions, 
technological diffusion within households, and user behavior [
4]. Incorporating these 
aspects into building stock simulations at a national scale is a challenging task due to the 
diversity of users and households and to the computational burden required to simulate 
millions of buildings. For instance, a recent paper used large public datasets to inform 
building energy models and study energy poverty in Southern Spain [
5].

This study not only provides a detailed representation of the Italian residential build-
ing stock but also serves as a valuable tool for identifying priority areas for energy 
efﬁciency interventions and renewable energy integration. The challenges of compu-
tational complexity have been addressed with a bottom-up model using data from an 
ISTA T survey on the energy consumption of families to account for the behavioral 
and technological diversity in energy use within households, providing high-resolution 
insights into heat demand patterns. Indeed, the dataset was previously used to develop 
a bottom-up national building stock model called MOIRAE in a collaborative effort 
between Politecnico di Milano and Ricerca Sistema Energetico [
6]. The simulation tool 
was later improved and released with the name MODENA [7], which allowed to perform 
a sensitivity analysis to study the effect of climate change on future energy consump-
tion [8]. The new model, called ita-domes, has been almost completely rewritten and is 
released as an open-source Github repository. 
2 Dataset 
This work relies on a dataset gathering detailed statistics on Italian households’ energy 
consumption in the form of responses to a survey, available online as open access data 
[9]. The target population targeted households with a landline subscription, selected ran-
domly across Italy’s 8,000 municipalities and stratiﬁed by demographic and geographic 
parameters (e.g., metropolitan centers, mountain towns, and coastal areas). The sampling 
was one-stage, including a base sample of 20,000 households with a reserve sample of 
100,000 additional households to ensure sufﬁcient participation. Surveys were conducted 
between March and July 2013 using CA TI (Computer-Assisted Telephone Interview-
ing), monitoring interviews to maintain high data quality. Households and respondents 
were selected carefully, engaging an adult in each household with substantial knowledge 
about energy-related topics. 
The survey includes more than 380 questions divided into nine sections: general 
information on households, household characteristics, heating system, domestic hot 
water, cooling system, biomass, lighting, household appliances, energy expenditure. 
These questions concern the presence, type, characteristics, number and type of use of 
different technologies related to the energy consumption of the households surveyed. 
By cross-referencing the answers with external databases or models, it was possible 
to characterize detailed energy consumption of households. To ensure data reliability, 
survey responses were cleaned and validated through deterministic and probabilistic 
correction methods. Errors were identiﬁed using ISTA T’s CONCORD software, which 
ensures coherence across responses.

de-scribed in Sect. 3.2, (iii) weather data for building simulations described in Sect. 3.3.

3.1 Models for space heating and cooling 
Space heating accounts for the highest energy demand in the Italian domestic sector. The 
calculation presented in this work relies on the lumped capacitance models implemented 
in the building energy simulation software EUReCA [
10], in particular with the one-
capacitance model proposed by ISO 13790 [ 11]. The input data for the simulations, 
including the stratigraphy and thermal properties of building components, have been 
selected from the answers to the survey described in Sect. 
2. The most important questions 
are summarized in Table 1. The combination of construction period, walls construction 
type and windows frame material resulted in sixty archetypes [ 12].

The ﬂoor surface area has been assumed equal to the rounded mean value of each 
class. For instance, if the answer was 40–59 m2, the considered surface area was assumed 
equal to 50 m2. The overall glazed surface has been calculated by multiplying the number 
of windows by an assumed average window area of 1.7 m 2. Oriented surface areas of 
external walls have been calculated accordingly based on surface exposure and assuming 
that all dwellings have a square ﬂoor plan. The boundary conditions of building surfaces 
depend on apartment position and surface exposure. The energy used for space heating 
and cooling depends not only on the building characteristics described above, but also on 
occupants’ behaviour. Therefore, hourly schedules of temperature setpoint and internal 
heat gains due to the occupants have been assumed based on the answers provided by 
each occupant. In particular, the heating setpoint schedule has been assumed based on 
the declared number of heating hours during morning, afternoon and evening time. The 
ﬁrst and last day of the heating season have been determined based on the climatic zone, 
according to the Italian law [
13].

Two methods have been used to calculate the electricity consumption of cooling 
devices. The ﬁrst one is the calculation of the cooling demand using ﬁxed indoor con-
ditions (usually 26 °C air temperature and 50% relative humidity). Then, the electricity 
consumption is calculated based on a given EER, which depends on average outdoor 
temperature. The second method multiplies a ﬁxed power by the number of daily hours 
estimated from the switch on frequency declared by the respondent (6 h/day if “always”, 
4 if “some days a week”, 3 if “once per week”, 0.75 if “less than 4 times per month”, 
0.5 if “occasionally”). 
The number of days with space cooling depends on the climatic zone of the respon-
dent. The emission, distribution, control, and generation efﬁciency were assumed for 
each dwelling considering the type of emitter, the system layout, and the age declared 
by the respondents. The only exception to this method is represented by heat pumps, 
whose efﬁciency is calculated dynamically using the correlation proposed by Staffell 
[
14].

annual number of days. The assumed power consumption is 15 W for energy-saving 
bulbs and 40 W for traditional bulbs, and three daily usage patterns are also assumed: 
3 h, 7 h, 14 h. 
Cooking devices are considered as thermal power, converting the obtained thermal 
energy demand to a certain consumption based on the device efﬁciency. For instance, 
100 h/year of use of a gas oven turn into 140 kWh/year of thermal energy produced by 
the oven to cook (average power 1.40 kW) with an efﬁciency of 40% leading to a gas 
consumption of 350 kWh/year (approximately 33 Smc/year). In a household where the 
same use frequency is declared for an electric oven (average power 1.5 kW and efﬁciency 
of 74%), 150 kWh/year of thermal energy produced by the oven become 203 kWh/year 
of electricity consumed by that device. 
The tool calculates the energy demand for domestic hot water (DHW) production 
using UNI/TS 11300–2 Standard [ 15]. 
3.3 Simulations 
The simulations performed to calculate the heating demand are based on typical mete-
orological years. These proﬁles have been developed by Comitato Termotecnico Ital-
iano starting from the weather data of 110 weather stations (one for each province) to 
inform the Italian Standard UNI 10349:2016 [
16] based on the methods contained in 
the International Standard EN ISO 15927-4 [ 17]. Therefore, the simulation uses typical 
meteorological years unless a speciﬁc year is speciﬁed by the user. 
For this study, weather data were selected for 2013 and 2014, relying on information 
collected by individual weather stations. This choice was made because the ISTA T 
survey on energy consumption of Italian households, which is the main input of our 
model, was carried out during 2013. Additionally, 2014 has been included because it 
was a particularly warm year, which makes it suitable to assess the model accuracy. 
In Italy there are 21 meteorological agencies: 19 regional agencies and those of the 
autonomous provinces of Trento and Bolzano. For this work, weather data of the typ-
ical meteorological year was overwritten using data from 30 meteorological stations, 
taken from Emilia Romagna (8 provinces), Friuli V enezia Giulia (4 provinces), Lazio 
(5 provinces), Lombardy (Milan only), Piemonte (4 provinces including Turin), the 
province of Trento, and V eneto (7 provinces). The considered provinces account for 
approximately 27% of the Italian households. The validation was performed at aggre-
gated (national) level on six energy carriers: electricity, natural gas, LPG, biomass (sum 
of wood and pellets), gasoline and district heating. The benchmark values were taken 
from the National Energy Balance (NEB).

4.1 Comparison with National Energy Balance 
Table 3 shows the overall national consumption by energy carrier with weather correc-
tions for 2013 and 2014 as speciﬁed in Sect. 3.3. Results about 2013 are very accurate for 
electricity and natural gas consumption. In the authors opinion, this result depends on the 
fact that weather data for 2013 are similar to the Test Reference Y ear from CTI. Indeed, 
2014 was much warmer. Since the weather data was replaced only for 30 provinces out 
of 110, the drop in natural gas consumption (−16% compared to 2013 according to 
the National Energy Balance) is not entirely captured by the model. The same happens 
for biomass consumption, although the slight underestimation of year 2013 is compen-
sated for by the warmer climate of 2014. The model overestimates LPG consumption, 
and underestimates gasoline and district heating supplied to households. For LPG and 
gasoline, the deviations from National Energy Balances could be linked to inaccurate 
assumptions on the energy demand for DHW and heating in some regions. For district 
heating, the error is most likely linked to insufﬁcient data in the ISTA T sample.

of heating degree days (HDD) and population, Fig.

related to space cooling. Lombardy shares the ﬁrst place with Sicily with approximately 
14% of the Italian consumption each, while the third and fourth energy consumers

4.3 Energy Consumption by Energy Carrier 
The heatmaps in Fig. 2 show the regional distribution of four energy carriers consumed 
annually in Italian households: electricity, natural gas, biomass and LPG. Electricity 
consumption is consistent with the results shown in the previous Section: the combination 
of energy demand due to appliances and cooling leads to Lombardy being the highest 
electricity consumer (17% of the National electricity consumption) followed by Lazio 
(10%) and Sicily (8.5%).

5 Conclusions 
The decarbonization of the building sector is a critical step toward achieving EU climate 
goals, with building simulations proving to be essential tools for evaluating energy 
efﬁciency and renewable integration at scale. This study demonstrates how a detailed, 
bottom-up model of the Italian residential building stock - developed using data from 
ISTA T surveys - can address challenges such as behavioral diversity and computational 
complexity. 
Results showed that energy use for space heating is mostly related to weather con-
ditions, population and available resources in case of biomass, while space cooling is 
inﬂuenced by other variables such as the presence of air conditioning systems.
