# Archetype campaign v2: candidate technical results

The new archetype implementation campaign is complete: 144 scientific annual cases and eight technical annual checks, 152 executions in total. It uses 32 held-out respondents and four shared real source-control components across four coordinate TMYs. These counts are computational cases, not 152 independently sampled households. No v1 archetype annual campaign or optional v2 replay was executed. All older campaigns and the input-only v1 singleton collapse remain preserved.

All four site gates passed, covering 1,331,520 exported hourly states. Maximum air/surface/mass residuals were 2.38e-11, 9.53e-11 and 1.77e-10 W; old-state continuity and repeat differences were exactly zero. Profile preservation, per-surface shortwave/longwave/glazing reconstruction, order invariance, solver forcing, finite full calendars, active setpoint adherence and zero cooling passed. Warm-up differences remained below the predeclared limits. These checks establish numerical consistency of the bounded study-local path; they do not establish empirical energy accuracy or replace an external building benchmark.

The conditional representation has lower overall descriptive RMSE in the eight endpoint/site comparisons shown above; these overall metrics use the eligible empirical group×branch weights. This does not hold for every subgroup. At Bolzano, individual annual-maximum RMSE is worse for archetype 2 (conditional 2.008767 versus pooled 1.965770 kW) and archetype 4 (2.941238 versus 2.929172 kW). Their paired MSE differences are positive, 0.170896 and 0.070831 kW², respectively. These predeclared subgroup counterexamples remain part of the interpretation alongside the favorable overall averages. This is a small, frozen, stratified held-out comparison against the same EUReCA model with detailed respondent inputs. Both methods use the same four separately simulated components, but group-conditioned probabilities have 12 free parameters versus three pooled. It is not an equal-total-complexity comparison, a measured-accuracy improvement, a demographic causal effect, or evidence of general algorithm superiority. Test weights describe the defined unweighted eligible empirical domain, not national household totals or survey-design confidence intervals.

The peak endpoint is the expectation of individual annual maximum hourly loads under each mixture. It is not a coincident group or urban peak. Duration preservation concerns high-setpoint placement under the source mapping; a lower assumed setback can still require heat, so reported block durations are not encoded actual heater runtime. The calendar and climate-E season are standardized across all sites, with source-seeded timing, fixed internal gains, fixed dwelling and the reviewed annual-mean longwave approximation. No occupancy/gain curve is invented from a demographic label.
